Loom feedback round 4: the Active section and the subagent panel

13 Oct 2026, after 0.4.2. Static mock-ups at real size (1 CSS px = 1 pt) with sample data. Chosen on 13 Oct (D-54): the subagent panel is P1. The other items of the round need no design choice: Stop and Resume no longer move a card, a stopped session keeps its conversation screen, a stopped session's tab closes, and the branch name gets more room.

1 · The Active section

The activity view gets a section above Today. A session is in it from the moment it starts working until you have read the result. After that it goes back to its day, by its last activity. The project view does not change.

  • In Active:
    • Working, including background work after the turn.
    • Needs approval, or a question asked during the turn.
    • A turn that finished or failed while you were not looking, until you read it.
  • Reading: selecting the session while the Loom window is in front.
    • The card stays in Active while it is selected, so it does not jump under the cursor.
    • It drops to its day when you move to another session.
    • Mark as Read drops it too.
  • Not in Active:
    • Ready, Idle, Stale and stopped sessions. Stop also closes the session's tab.
    • A just-resumed session, until a prompt starts work.
    • The "Waiting for you" that Claude shows about a minute after a turn you already read, so a read session is not pulled back.
  • Order: newest activity first, as in the rest of the list. A prompt or a finished turn brings a card to the top of Active. A state change alone (approval, Working) does not move it.
  • Heading: "Active" with the count, in the day heading's style.
    • It is not drawn when nothing is active.
    • It is not drawn while searching.

2 · The subagent panel

A click on a subagent row opens that subagent's transcript, live while it runs, in Claude's own style. It is read-only: approvals are still answered in the session's terminal, and a notice says so.

  • What it shows:
    • The brief, clipped.
    • The agent's text.
    • Each tool call with its main argument, and a one-line result. Errors are red.
    • "Thought for 12s" where it thought. Claude does not write the thinking text to disk.
  • How it updates: per block, as Claude writes the file, not per token.
  • What it costs: Loom watches the file only while the panel is on screen, reads only the new lines, and keeps the last few hundred entries. Nothing runs for a panel you are not looking at.

Ink

Loom's next design language. It keeps what Loom already is: the sidebar, the header, the inspector, the status marks and the periwinkle. What changes is the surface. One calm canvas, one family of controls, and a little art where nothing needs reading.

01

One canvas

The window is one surface. Hairlines divide it and nothing is boxed twice. Only what you act on is lifted: the composer, a menu, a card.

02

Two voices

SF Pro for what you read. SF Mono for what the machine says: branches, paths, times, tokens, counts and keys.

03

Colour means state

The chrome is grey. Periwinkle marks focus and the default action. Orange, blue and red are status only. A project's tone is its mark and the tint of its tabs and its selected session.

04

One control family

One height, radius, hover and focus ring for every control. Session tabs are pills and view tabs are underlined, wherever they appear.

05

Two reading points

The sidebar tells you how every session is doing. The header tells you how this one is doing and what you can do. Everything else is detail.

06

A school of sharks

The sharks are the identity: a hammerhead, a thresher and a whale shark, drawn in ASCII. They swim on empty canvases and while something waits, never behind the terminal or a list.

06

The school

The sharks are Loom's identity, not decoration. The hammerhead is the main character, and a thresher and a whale shark swim with it. Each has a place in the app's flow, and none of them ever swims behind something you are reading.

Hammerhead the main character

Home's band, a new session and About. Later, the app's icon and the menu bar mark.

Thresher the quick one

Its tail is as long as its body. It crosses Home now and then and marks an empty Changes tab.

Whale shark the slow giant

It swims deep below a new session and marks an empty Side tab. Its spots are the only pattern in the app.

The ASCII tick in every status slot: the sidebar, the tabs, the header, the inspector.

07

What to react to

Round two. A yes, a no or "more like X" on each is enough.

  1. Project colour. Marks, tinted tabs and a tinted selection. Strong enough now, or too much anywhere?
  2. The branches. One per row, the last one curving. Right weight and spacing?
  3. The spinner. Its speed and weight in the sidebar, the tabs and the header.
  4. The cast. Hammerhead, thresher and whale shark. Keep these two companions, or another (a sawshark, a reef shark)?
  5. Where they swim. Anything to add to the flow, or to take out of it?

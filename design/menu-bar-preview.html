Loom menu bar item, menu, notification and Settings rows · design preview

12 Oct 2026, hardening round 1, slice H1-F (D-49). Static mock-ups drawn at real size (1 CSS pixel = 1 pt), not screenshots. The menu bar is 24 pt high and the mark 18 pt. Menu, banner and Notification Center are system surfaces, drawn from memory of macOS 27 (glass, large radii); their exact look is the system's. Every status colour is the Identity A value; where a light menu bar needs another value it says so. Nothing here is decided until the user picks.

1 · Menu bar item states, dark and light menu bar, at 1× with neighbours and enlarged 6×

The mark is Loom-menubar.svg (single colour). Idle is the plain template image: black on a light bar, white on a dark one. The badge slot is the mark's lower right corner. It holds either the working chase (option A, 9.6 pt, the sidebar chase at 0.8 scale, same 8 dots, 10 steps per second) or one status dot (7 pt), never both, and the mark is cut away around it so the badge sits on the bar, not on the sheep. Option B drops the mark while working and shows the chase alone at 16 pt (1.33 scale). The dot colour is the most urgent state among the sessions that need you: failure red, then attention orange, then unviewed blue (the order the project rows use). A coloured dot means the status item is no longer a pure template image: Loom draws the item itself (a drawing-handler image that follows the bar's appearance) so the mark still turns black or white.

Planner: option A, the mark with the chase badge. It keeps the identity visible in every state, the badge slot is shared with the status dot (so working, then needs you, swaps one thing in one place), and it reads as the sidebar's own indicator. Option B is a larger, clearer motion, but the bar loses the Loom mark for as long as anything works, which in daily use is most of the day. A light menu bar needs darker status values (orange #B05A00, blue #2A5FD6, red #C9332E; the brand hues are only 1.4 to 2.2:1 on a light bar, these are 3.4 to 3.9:1); that is the one place Loom shows a status colour on a light surface.

Accessibility

Increase Contrast: the dot grows from 7 to 8 pt and takes a stronger hue (lighter on a dark bar, darker on a light one), and the chase trail's floor goes from 16 % to 45 % so the tail stays visible. Reduce Motion (proposal, rows below): the chase freezes on its first frame, the head dot and the trail as drawn. The status item always carries an accessibility label: “Loom”, “Loom, 2 sessions working”, “Loom, 1 session needs you, 2 working”.

2 · The menu NSMenu under the item, dark and light, with overflow and empty cases

Two sections, shown only when they have sessions: Needs you (status dot, session title, project in secondary text) and Working (the chase). Each lists at most 5; more shows a final “and N more…” row that opens Loom. Order inside Needs you: failure, then attention, then unviewed, most recent first within a class (the project rows' order); Working: most recent first. A click on a session opens Loom and selects it. Then New Claude Session, Open Loom, a separator, Settings… and Quit Loom. The highlighted row in the first menu is the system accent (shown as the default blue; the menu follows the user's accent colour). A long title ellipsizes at its end; the project keeps its width (up to 112 pt).

Planner: as drawn: one line per session, the project trailing in secondary text (an NSMenuItem with an attributed title and a tab stop, a 12 pt status image). Two-line rows would double the height of a menu that already reaches 15 rows when busy. Section headers are the system's section-header items. “and N more…” is a plain item (it just brings Loom forward, where the sidebar has the rest). The empty case keeps the menu useful (New Claude Session, Open Loom) and says why it is otherwise empty in one disabled line. The shortcuts are the app menu's own (Cmd-N, Cmd-comma, Cmd-Q); a status menu shows them but they only fire while Loom is active.

3 · Notification “Needs approval” banner and Notification Center

Sent when a session turns Needs approval while Loom is not the active app. App icon on the left, title = the session title, body = “<project> · Claude is waiting for your approval.”. No sound, no action buttons (an approval is answered in the session's terminal, so the only action is opening it). A click on the banner opens Loom and selects that session. When the approval is answered anywhere else (in Loom, from another window, or the session ends), Loom removes the notification from Notification Center. Nothing is sent for failures, finished sessions or sessions that start working.

Planner: as drawn. Default interruption level (not Time Sensitive), so Focus and Do Not Disturb keep their meaning and no sound plays. The title carries the session, not the words “Needs approval”; the body says it. Grouping is the system default by app: every Loom approval stacks under one “Loom” group, newest on top, and clearing the group clears them all. Grouping by project (one stack per project) is possible with a thread identifier, but it adds nothing for the user's typical three to five sessions. The first time Loom asks, macOS shows its own permission prompt (the Settings row below says so); a denial leaves the switch on but inert, and the row says where to change it.

4 · Settings → General three new rows in a second card

The existing window, 680 × 520, drawn at real size. The new rows sit in a second card under the existing one (font size, Project colors), so General reads as “how Loom looks” then “how Loom reaches you”. Show in menu bar (default on) shows the item of section 1 and its menu; off removes it entirely. Notify when a session needs approval (default on) controls section 3. Open at login (default off) registers Loom as a login item; macOS may list it in System Settings → General → Login Items.

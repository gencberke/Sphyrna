Loom per-project colours · design preview

12 Oct 2026. Static mock-ups, not screenshots, drawn on the real window geometry and the Identity A tokens (sidebar tint 0.6 since D-44). Nothing here is decided until the user picks. Every section ends with a space for the planner's recommendation.

0 · Palette six soft tones, each checked against the status colours

Each project takes one tone from a fixed palette. A tone has four parts: a Candy pill fill and text, a Glass tint and text, and one stripe colour (the same colour as the Glass tint, drawn at full strength). The two pill renderings are the user's sketch (the project name in an oval in the project's colour, with a darker shade of the same colour as text) built in the two ways that can work on Loom's dark sidebar. Darker text only stays readable on a light pill: on the dark sidebar a dark-on-translucent pill would sink into the background, so Candy needs an opaque pastel fill, while Glass keeps the dark-mode-native look and flips to lighter text.

Numbers below are computed by this page from the colours it draws (WCAG 2.x contrast; hue is the HSL hue). Translucent fills are composited over the sidebar #0B0E16 and over the worst case: the real sidebar (tint 0.6) over a 0.5 grey backdrop, #3A3B40, the same case LoomThemeTests uses (sidebarOverWhite). Targets: text ≥ 4.5:1, stripe ≥ 3:1, hue distance ≥ 30° from attention orange, unviewed blue, failure red and the accent.

Next to the status language

Status and accent
Project tones

Status is always a dot or the chase, 8 pt or 12 pt, in the indicator slot or at the attention-dot position. A project tone is never a dot: it is a rounded rectangle (pill, stripe, underline, swatch) and never sits in those positions. Even pastel Aqua (38° from the unviewed blue) and Lemon (36° from attention orange) stay separate by hue and by shape.

Planner: keep these six. Rose is the tightest (32° from the failure red), but a project colour is never a dot, so it stays. A new project takes the least-used tone (ties in palette order); any project can switch to another tone or None. Past six projects two share a tone, which the name next to it resolves.

6 · Accessibility Increase Contrast, Reduce Transparency, Differentiate Without Color

Increase Contrast (see the existing rule in DESIGN.md §6: hairline 30 %, secondary text #A9AEBF): the stripe and the underline get wider (3 → 4 pt, 2 → 3 pt) and lighter, Candy text goes darker, Glass text goes lighter and the Glass pill gains a 1 pt outline in its tone (the tint stays at 22 %: more tint would lower the text contrast). Reduce Transparency already makes the sidebar opaque; the Glass pill then becomes the opaque equivalent, its tint pre-composited over #0B0E16, so nothing of the pill depends on what is behind the window. For each tone, left sample = normal, right sample = changed.

Differentiate Without Color

Colour is never the only carrier of the project. In the sidebar the project name is always written next to the colour (the header's name in every view, the project label on every activity card), so the stripe, the pill tint and the underline are redundant cues. Tabs carry the project in the tooltip and in the accessibility label. Status keeps its own rule (dots differ by colour only today; DP-I1 is still open) and is not changed by this feature.
